# Related work for the six candidate bottlenecks A–F (literature search, 2026-11-14)

Scope: generic web search (arXiv / ACL / NeurIPS / ICLR pages). Every paper below was found in this search and has a URL. Most details come from abstract pages or search snippets, not full-text reading. Items marked **[unverified]** rest on secondary summaries or need a full-text check.

Setting being compared against: Qwen3-32B, paper title → author list; graded recognition labels (multiple choice), scoring of every author token, title-specific contrast (true title vs false title / no title).

## Short notes per bottleneck

**A – prior competition.** The idea of subtracting a "no-context" distribution to remove the model's prior is well established: CAD (Shi et al.) is exactly logits(with title) − logits(without title), and domain-conditional PMI (Holtzman et al.) is the scoring-time version for answer selection. Contrastive Decoding (Li et al.) is the expert-minus-amateur variant. Knowledge overshadowing (Zhang et al. 2024/2025) gives the matching mechanism: popular knowledge suppresses rarer knowledge, and they also fix it with self-contrastive decoding. Zhao et al. 2026 report that authors named in hallucinated references are more productive and more cited than controls, which fits "frequent names win" in real fabricated citations (abstract-level, aggregate). What remains new: a false-title control (does the gain come from *this* title?), the person-name domain, all-token scoring, and evaluation stratified by recognition grade. Pre-registration should cite CAD/PMI as the method source and claim novelty only for the diagnostic use.

**B – format lock.** No direct prior work was found on whether the output format (given name first vs surname first, surnames only, initial completion) gates recall of author names. Indirect support: the reversal curse and Calderon et al. (reverse direction hurts recall but not multiple-choice recognition) show that retrieval depends on direction and cue; the depth-use preprint finds the first token of a multi-token answer is the hardest step; surface form competition shows probability split across spellings. This looks like the most clearly open bottleneck.

**C – neighbour confusion.** Prior work documents that fabricated references contain inconsistent author lists (Agrawal et al.), conflation of overlapping records (Niimi: "spurious-attractor interference"), better coauthor reconstruction for visible scholars (Kalhor & Mashhadi), and confusion among similar scientists as biased latent inference (Hu et al., ScientistQA). Claims that fake references often use real same-field names come from commentary and secondary sources **[unverified]**. A quantitative test of whether fabricated names sit in the true authors' co-author or field neighbourhood was not found. Partly done.

**D – name chain.** Yan & Jia (EMNLP 2025) give the mechanism for one-to-many recall (answers already generated condition the promotion of new ones), and work on cued recall / in-context exemplars shows facilitation. But a retrieval-induced-forgetting result (ICML 2026, details not checked) shows that related-item cues can also *suppress* recall. No paper was found that runs a true-vs-false first-author cue experiment. Open; both directions are plausible a priori, which argues for a two-sided pre-registered hypothesis.

**E – readout bottleneck.** This is the best-covered bottleneck. Inside-Out (Gekhman et al., COLM 2025) shows on closed-book QA that internal scoring finds about 40% more knowledge than output-based measures, and that some correct answers are practically never sampled although they would be ranked first. That is close to the "knows but cannot say" framing. Orgad et al. show correct answers are encoded while wrong ones are generated. Halawi et al. show logit-lens early exit can beat the final layer. DoLa, SLED and activation decoding use intermediate layers at decoding time; the tuned lens is the standard readout tool; ITI is the steering analogue. Novelty here is limited to the domain (multi-token person names, bibliographic data), lens-based re-ranking of a candidate set, and stratification by recognition grade. The pre-registration should cite Inside-Out as the closest precedent and frame E as a replication and extension.

**F – exposure threshold.** Frequency/popularity effects on recall are established (Kandpal et al.; Mallen et al. PopQA), and for bibliographic data specifically Niimi finds log-linear accuracy in citation count with thresholds (about 90 / about 1,000–1,250 citations; the figures differ between preprint versions; one closed model; n = 100). Calderon et al. separate encoding from recall and find failures concentrated on long-tail facts; the Benchmark Illusion preprint defines "recognition-only" items. No study found estimates separate exposure thresholds for recognition and recall on the same paper→author items. Partly done; the paired-threshold claim is new.

## Additional context (general "hidden knowledge" framing)
- Calderon et al. 2026, "Empty Shelves or Lost Keys?" https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14080 — the closest high-level framing to the whole study (encoded but not recallable; thinking recovers part of it). Preprint, no venue listed.
- Gekhman et al. 2025, "Inside-Out" https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15299 — the closest method-level precedent for "knows more than it says".
- Naser 2026, cross-model audit of reference fabrication (10 LLMs, 69,557 citations; 11.4–56.8% hallucination) https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03299 — no author-name breakdown in the abstract.

## Caveats
- Details are mostly abstract-level; venues for some 2025–2026 preprints were not confirmed.
- The ICML 2026 retrieval-induced-forgetting paper was seen only as a conference listing (authors not recorded here).
- Zhao et al. 2026 author-prominence result and the "real same-field names" claims for fabricated references should be checked in full text before being cited as evidence.
- Not searched in depth: tokenizer-level studies of person names, name-order effects for non-Western names, and early-exit work dedicated to entity names.
