## Task 1: text-model baseline for G'(l) = cos(orig, para) - cos(orig, D)

Each text (original title, Qwen paraphrase, lexical neighbour D, partner B) encoded alone, no prompt; 1137 papers, 3949 unique texts. bert-base-uncased: hidden layers 0..12 (L0 = embedding layer = the text baseline, analogous to Qwen L4), positions title_last (last wordpiece of the title; the token before [SEP], often the final "." when the title ends with one, as in Qwen), mean_title (mean of the title wordpieces) and cls. all-mpnet-base-v2: hidden layers 0..12, positions mean (the model's own pooling: mask-mean over all tokens incl. <s>, </s>), title_last, cls; layer "final" (13 in the CSV) = SentenceTransformer sentence embedding (identical in cosine to L12 mean: max |cos - 1| = 2.4e-07). Qwen3-32B: title_last from X3 (base L4).

**Caveat:** all-mpnet-base-v2 was trained (contrastively) on > 1 billion sentence pairs from large web and scientific corpora (incl. S2ORC title-abstract / citation pairs, SPECTER pairs), so it may have seen many of these titles, but it never saw author identities as a target. Raw cosine differences are not on a common scale across models (anisotropy, dimension), hence the ratio max_l (G'(l) - G'(base)) / G'(base) as the scale-free comparison. [CLS] / <s> at L0 is the same vector for every text, so its G'(L0) = 0 and the cls ratio is shown only relative to L1 (and is NaN for mpnet, whose <s> is still constant at L1).

## Conclusion

1. **BERT (bert-base-uncased) shows no middle-layer advantage**: on the primary test set G'(l) falls below its embedding-layer value at every layer (title_last: L0 +0.495, then -0.06 to -0.21; mean_title -0.08 to -0.18; also negative relative to L1); ratio -0.12 / -0.25 vs Qwen +0.52 [0.46, 0.59] (paired difference +0.64 [0.58, 0.71]). Its [CLS] rises from 0 to +0.13 (L11), but [CLS] has no text baseline (constant at L0), so it is not comparable.
2. **all-mpnet-base-v2 gains only at its output layer, not in the middle**: mean pooling dips to -0.15..-0.19 below L0 at L1-L10 and rises to +0.142 [0.129, 0.156] only at L12 / the sentence embedding (ratio 0.30 [0.27, 0.33]); title_last +0.09 at L12 (ratio 0.17). Qwen's middle-layer ratio is larger (Qwen - mpnet mean +0.22 [0.16, 0.30]); same in the sensitivity set and on all papers.
3. **Caveat from the matched-D subset (D at least as word-similar as the paraphrase; test n = 37)**: here a paraphrase-trained text model also separates paraphrase from D well beyond its baseline (mpnet mean final +0.31, ratio 1.40 [1.11, 1.73]; title_last rises from L2) - comparable to Qwen (+0.22 at L36, ratio 1.90 [0.93, 5.44]; difference CI includes 0). So "paraphrase closer than a word-matched neighbour" is reachable by meaning similarity alone; Qwen's distinctive feature is the middle-layer timing (L28-L40, falling again by L60), not the size of the effect.
4. **Behaviour recovery** (false = lexical neighbour D, because Stage 10 false_0 used a prompt with the year): the paraphrase keeps about 90% of the original's first-author advantage over D - primary test 0.90 [0.87, 0.94]; target 0.90 [0.85, 0.94], control 0.91 [0.84, 0.98], target - control -0.01 [-0.09, +0.07]; sensitivity and all papers the same; partner B as false 0.92. Matched-D subset lower, ~0.73-0.77 with wide CIs.
5. Caveats: cosine differences are not on a common scale across models (hence the ratios); mpnet may have seen these titles in training (not the authors); for control papers the true - false gap is small (0.18 vs 0.35 for target, 19-26% of control papers have true <= false), so per-paper ratios are unstable and only ratios of means are reported.
